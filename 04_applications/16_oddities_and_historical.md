## Numbers stations and the unexplained

Since the 1950s, shortwave has carried transmissions consisting of nothing but strings of
numbers, letters or tones — read by synthesised or human voices, endlessly, to no acknowledged
recipient. They are widely understood to be **one-time-pad messages to intelligence agents**: the
one cryptographic scheme that is provably unbreakable, and which requires no equipment at the
receiving end beyond an ordinary radio and a pad of paper.

Nobody has ever been prosecuted for listening. Nobody has ever officially confirmed what they are.

| Signal | Frequency | Character | Diff | Needs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **"The Buzzer" (UVB-76 / MDZhB)** | 4625 kHz | Continuous buzzing, occasional voice | ⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 Broadcasting since the 1970s. Occasionally interrupted by Russian voice messages |
| **"The Pip"** | 3756, 5448 kHz | Repeating short tone | ⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 |
| **"The Squeaky Wheel"** | 3828, 5473 kHz | Rhythmic squeak | ⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 |
| **E11 "Oblique"** | various HF | English female voice, groups of 5 | ⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 |
| **E07 / S06 families** | various HF | Voice, numeric groups | ⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 |
| **M12 / M14** | various HF | Morse-based numbers | ⭐⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 |
| **XPA / XPA2 ("Polytone")** | various HF | PSK burst transmissions | ⭐⭐⭐ | 🔻📡 | 🟢 Modern digital successors |
| **"Lincolnshire Poacher" (retired)** | HF | English folk tune + numbers | — | — | 🟢 Ceased 2008. Recordings survive |
| **"Swedish Rhapsody" (retired)** | HF | Music box + child's voice | — | — | 🟢 Ceased 1998 |

**The Priyom.org database** catalogues schedules and formats. Tuning in to the Buzzer, hearing
that endless drone, and knowing it has continued uninterrupted for fifty years is an experience
no digital decode can match.

## Try this first: the Buzzer

If you have an upconverter, tune to **4625 kHz USB** and just listen.

You will hear a monotonous buzzing tone, repeating roughly 25 times a minute. It has been doing
this, from a transmitter in Russia, since the 1970s. Every few weeks it stops and a voice reads a
short string of Russian words and numbers, then the buzzing resumes.

Nobody outside the operating organisation knows what it is for.

It is a useful reminder that the spectrum is not merely a set of protocols to be decoded. Some of
what is out there is genuinely strange, and listening to it — not decoding, just listening — is
part of the hobby too.

## Then: hear an NDB before they are gone

Non-directional beacons are the oldest radio navigation aid still operating, and they are being
decommissioned worldwide as GNSS replaces them. Each is a plain carrier with a two- or
three-letter Morse identifier repeating forever, at powers from 25 W to a few kW, somewhere
between 190 and 535 kHz.

Tune with a **narrow CW filter**, listen for the ident, and look it up. NDB DXers routinely log
beacons over 2000 km away at night, using nothing but a loop of wire and patience.

In ten years most of them will be silent. It is a small, quiet corner of the spectrum, and it is
closing.
