dB is a comparison. dBm is an actual power. dBFS is how full the converter is.

The one to hammer is dBFS — a ceiling you must not hit, because clipping is a failure mode they have never had to watch for on an ordinary radio. Their car radio never told them the input stage was overloading.

Three units you will see all afternoon

dB

A comparison. "Twice as strong" is 3 dB. "Ten times" is 10 dB. Always relative to something else.

dBm

An actual power. Absolute, not relative. A strong FM station at your antenna might be −40 dBm.

dBFS

How full the converter is. 0 dBFS is completely full. This is a ceiling you must not hit.

Hit 0 dBFS and the signal is clipped — flattened, distorted, unrecoverable. Your car radio never had to warn you about this. Today you watch for it.

Turning gain up amplifies the noise too, and past a point only adds distortion.

It is the mistake every newcomer makes on day one. Say this before Segment 5, because they will reach for the slider the moment they see one.

The five mistakes everyone makes

  1. Gain at maximum. It amplifies the noise too.
  2. No antenna, or the wrong one. The cheapest part that matters most.
  3. Wrong sample rate. Everything downstream is then wrong.
  4. Expecting a weak signal indoors. Buildings are very good at blocking radio.
  5. Skipping the fundamentals — then blaming the radio.
